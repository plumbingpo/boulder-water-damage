Wet Carpet Drying: What Boulder Homeowners Should Know

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. Our team in Boulder extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Warning Signs

  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
  • A floor that squeaks or feels soft under the carpet where it got wet
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Stains From Furniture

Furniture legs sitting on wet carpet release wood stain and rust into the fibers. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Mold on the Backing

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Water that started clean does not stay that way. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Damp Base of the Walls

Water that runs under the carpet reaches the base of the walls. It wicks up into the baseboards and drywall unnoticed, since the carpet hides that edge.

Our Process

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Day 3 to 7

Carpet Starts Failing

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

After a week

Past Saving

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.
