How to Waterproof Your Basement in Boulder, CO

Home / Blog / Basement Waterproofing Guide in Boulder, CO

Your home's structural integrity, indoor air quality, and resale value all depend on keeping the basement dry. Moisture below grade is common across Boulder, CO, whether that means slight seepage or a major flood. Below, Boulder Restoration explains why water gets into basements and which solutions reliably keep a Boulder basement dry.

What Makes a Basement Wet

There is more than one route for water to reach a basement, so the first job is to find out which one you have. Soaked soil presses on the foundation, and that hydrostatic pressure drives moisture through any crack, joint, or pore in the concrete. Foundation walls can also crack under the lateral push of expanding clay, which gives water a way inside. If the yard slopes the wrong way, gutters are blocked, or drainage is inadequate, rain collects at the foundation and works its way inside. Finally, there is condensation: cool surfaces in a basement pull moisture out of warm humid air, leaving things damp although nothing is leaking.

Outside Fixes for a Wet Basement

Long term, nothing works as well as handling the water outside, before it reaches the wall. A crew excavates along the foundation, puts a waterproof membrane coating on the wall, and then installs new exterior drain tile or upgrades what is there. No other method costs as much, though none protects as thoroughly, since the water is stopped where it starts. Regrading is also worth the money, because ground that falls 1 inch or more per foot across the first 6 feet away from the foundation carries water off.

Waterproofing From the Inside

With an interior system, the goal is to collect and move water after it enters, not to block it outside. Interior French drain systems collect water along the perimeter of the basement and send it to a sump pump for discharge. During heavy rainfall and outages, a sump system does the work, and a battery backup is the ideal setup. For significant water intrusion you still need drainage, since coatings and sealants on the inside only offer limited help with efflorescence and small amounts of seepage.

Keeping Your Sump Pump Ready

If your Boulder home has a sump pump, keep up with its maintenance. Four times a year, pour a bucket of water in the sump pit and watch for the pump to start. Odd noises while it runs can signal a mechanical problem. Install a battery backup to stay protected during power outages, the most common time for basement flooding. For more protection still, think about a second, water-driven pump. Make sure the discharge line sends water at least 6 feet from your foundation and has a check valve to prevent backflow.
